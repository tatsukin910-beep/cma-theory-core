## 1. Biography

**Tatsuki Nakamura** is an independent lived-experience researcher based in Shiga, Japan, specializing in the structural and environmental analysis of long-term abuse in institutional and closed domestic contexts. Drawing from his background as a former care worker and an abuse survivor living with neurodevelopmental and physical disabilities, his work bridges firsthand lived experience with formal structural theory.

He developed the **Complex Multilayered Abuse (CMA)** framework—a non-diagnostic, structural model designed to explain why abuse becomes chronic, invisible, and self-sustaining across closed interpersonal dynamics and institutional blind spots. His research initiative has been officially selected for Phase 2 of the **IFRC (International Federation of Red Cross and Red Crescent Societies) Limitless 2026** Youth Innovation Academy, conducted in collaboration with and supported by the Japanese Red Cross Society.

## 2. Research Focus & Theoretical Architecture

### Complex Multilayered Abuse (CMA) Framework
The CMA framework moves beyond individual psychopathology, medical diagnostics, or subjective narratives of distress. Instead, it systematically models the progressive structural capture of victims across five interrelated layers:

1. **Isolation & Information Deprivation (Layer 1):** Physical and epistemic severance from outside benchmarks.
2. **Structural Dependence & Economic Binding (Layer 2):** Institutional or financial lock-in preventing autonomous disengagement.
3. **Normalization & Systematic Gaslighting (Layer 3):** Cognitive distortion where abusive dynamics are reframed as necessary care, discipline, or mutual consensus.
4. **Institutional Non-Responsiveness (Layer 4):** Administrative blind spots, jurisdictional gaps, and structural inaction from formal welfare and legal systems.
5. **Embodiment & Sensory/Memory Fragmentation (Layer 5):** Somatization, autonomic dysregulation, and chronified survival reflexes.
